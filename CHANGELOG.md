# Changelog

## 0.1.0 (unreleased)

### Added

- `rustlink-collections`: `LinkedList`, `BTreeMap`/`BTreeSet` and `HashMap`/`HashSet` in one crate, `#![no_std]` with `alloc` and no `unsafe`. The list is one dense `Vec` with swap-removal; the map is a B+tree whose leaves are chained in order and whose inner nodes hold separators and `W`-wide `OptAbs` links; the hash map chains entries through `W`-wide links in buckets, reuses slots through a free list, grows at a three-quarter load factor and defaults to FNV-1a. `K` needs `Clone` for the B+tree and `Default` for the hash map. Widths that cannot address another node return the value in `Full` from the `try_` methods and panic from the plain ones.
- `rustlink-bench`: seven dynamic-container workloads (`btree-lookup`, `btree-scan`, `btree-churn`, `hash-lookup`, `hash-scan`, `hash-churn`, `list-scan`) comparing `std`, `std-fnv`, `u16` and `u32` variants on build, lookup, scan and churn; per-repetition thread CPU time from `CLOCK_THREAD_CPUTIME_ID`, printed next to wall time, with build and traverse CPU tables in the report. A plain `sweep` now runs 913 cells.
- `bench/containers/`: the record of those workloads (144 cells: 129 done, 15 skipped because 16-bit links cannot address the size): 14 complete sweeps, per-cell medians, all replicates and a ratios table with the same-hasher control. Heap per entry is 25 bytes for the hash map against 34 for std's (21 with 16-bit links) and 16 for the list against 24; the 16-bit hash map is limited to 65,535 entries. The hash map's scan is 2.3x to 3.6x faster than std's with the same hasher, the list's build is 9x to 10x faster, and the list's scan is 1.4x to 1.6x slower in cache but faster at 2^18. The dynamic B+tree uses 1.41x std's memory and is 0.87x to 1.23x std's lookup time from 2^14 to 2^20, so a dynamic ordered map is where narrow links do not pay with 8-byte values.

- `rustlink`: narrow typed links `Abs`, `OptAbs`, `Rel` and `OptRel` at `u8`, `u16`, `u32` and `u64`; bounds-checked resolution through `Region` and `Cursor`; the append-only `Arena`; `Links` and `impl_links!`; position-independent blobs with `Arena::freeze` and `BlobRef::verify`. `#![no_std]`, no `unsafe`.
- `rustlink-bench`: pointer, `usize`, `u32` and rustlink variants of binary search trees, an expression tree, graphs and blobs; the `rlb` one-cell runner; the `sweep` matrix driver; a Markdown report, in registry order, with the layer-4 gate table.
- `bench/`: the recorded sweep of the nine original workloads (405 cells, the whole matrix before the node graphs and B-trees below were added): `results.md` and `results.jsonl`, `replicates.tsv` with the six sweeps behind every measured cell, `box-paired.tsv` and `box-refgated.tsv` with the paired runs and the reference-gated attempts for the pointer variant, and `environment.txt` on the machine, the selection rule and the noise. On trees, 32-bit rustlink links halve the heap of 64-bit pointers and are 1.3x to 2.3x faster where memory dominates; on linked lists narrow links save memory (up to 3x) but not time; sub-32-bit links fit only local structures; the layer-4 gate passes (3.68x less heap at 0.81x to 0.84x of the traversal time). The 14 `box` cells of the two linked-list graph workloads are skipped until they can be measured on an idle machine.
- `rustlink-bench`: six more workloads. `ngraph-mesh`, `ngraph-banded`, `ngraph-random` and `ngraph-chase` are node graphs of a `u32` payload and four links, with `Rc` + `Weak`, `usize`, `u32` and every rustlink width, plus the probes `abs32-idx` and `rel16-idx` that decode with `Link::target` and index the slice without `Cursor`; `btree-64` and `btree-256` are packed, bulk-loaded B+trees with `std::collections::BTreeSet`, `box`, `usize`, `u32`, `abs32` and `abs16` variants. The report prints the layer-4 gate only when the results contain the expression tree. They are registered like the others, so a plain `sweep` now runs 769 cells (405 original and 364 new); `--workloads` selects, and the 405 and the 364 are the two records.
- `bench/decisive/`: the record of those workloads (364 cells): 37 repetitions of the matrix, judged on the calm ones, with `results.md`, `results.jsonl`, `replicates.tsv`, `sweeps.tsv`, `ratios.tsv` and `environment.txt`. Against `Rc` + `Weak` nodes, 32-bit links are 3.2x smaller and faster from 2^16 nodes up; against hand-written `u32` indices they cost 1.2x to 2.4x in sweeps and nothing measurable in dependent chases and B-tree lookups; `rel16` beats `u32` on memory (12 bytes per node against 20) and time (0.80x to 0.83x) on large banded graphs, and elsewhere it fits takes 1.5x to 2.4x the `u32` time in sweeps and 1.0x to 1.2x in the small dependent chases it reaches; link width barely moves a B-tree. Of eight predictions written beforehand, two held (heap bytes, reach), two held with narrow misses, one held half, one was not decided and two were falsified (`rel16` in a mesh sweep, `BTreeSet` within 1.5x of a packed B-tree); `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-23-decisive-workloads-design.md` has the table.
