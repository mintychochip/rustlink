# Changelog

## 0.1.0 (unreleased)

### Added

- `rustlink`: narrow typed links `Abs`, `OptAbs`, `Rel` and `OptRel` at `u8`, `u16`, `u32` and `u64`; bounds-checked resolution through `Region` and `Cursor`; the append-only `Arena`; `Links` and `impl_links!`; position-independent blobs with `Arena::freeze` and `BlobRef::verify`. `#![no_std]`, no `unsafe`.
- `rustlink-bench`: pointer, `usize`, `u32` and rustlink variants of binary search trees, an expression tree, graphs and blobs; the `rlb` one-cell runner; the `sweep` matrix driver; a Markdown report, in registry order, with the layer-4 gate table.
- `bench/`: the recorded sweep of the default matrix (405 cells): `results.md` and `results.jsonl`, `replicates.tsv` with the six sweeps behind every measured cell, `box-paired.tsv` and `box-refgated.tsv` with the paired runs and the reference-gated attempts for the pointer variant, and `environment.txt` on the machine, the selection rule and the noise. On trees, 32-bit rustlink links halve the heap of 64-bit pointers and are 1.3x to 2.3x faster where memory dominates; on linked lists narrow links save memory (up to 3x) but not time; sub-32-bit links fit only local structures; the layer-4 gate passes (3.68x less heap at 0.81x to 0.84x of the traversal time). The 14 `box` cells of the two linked-list graph workloads are skipped until they can be measured on an idle machine.
- `rustlink-bench`: six more workloads. `ngraph-mesh`, `ngraph-banded`, `ngraph-random` and `ngraph-chase` are node graphs of a `u32` payload and four links, with `Rc` + `Weak`, `usize`, `u32` and every rustlink width, plus the probes `abs32-idx` and `rel16-idx` that decode with `Link::target` and index the slice without `Cursor`; `btree-64` and `btree-256` are packed, bulk-loaded B+trees with `std::collections::BTreeSet`, `box`, `usize`, `u32`, `abs32` and `abs16` variants. The report prints the layer-4 gate only when the results contain the expression tree.
- `bench/decisive/`: the record of those workloads (364 cells): 37 repetitions of the matrix, judged on the calm ones, with `results.md`, `results.jsonl`, `replicates.tsv`, `sweeps.tsv`, `ratios.tsv` and `environment.txt`. Against `Rc` + `Weak` nodes, 32-bit links are 3.2x smaller and faster from 2^16 nodes up; against hand-written `u32` indices they cost 1.2x to 2.4x in sweeps and nothing measurable in dependent chases and B-tree lookups; `rel16` beats `u32` on memory (12 bytes per node against 20) and time (0.80x to 0.83x) on large banded graphs, and elsewhere it fits takes 1.5x to 2.4x the `u32` time in sweeps and 1.0x to 1.2x in the small dependent chases it reaches; link width barely moves a B-tree. Of eight predictions written beforehand, two held (heap bytes, reach), two held with narrow misses, one held half, one was not decided and two were falsified (`rel16` in a mesh sweep, `BTreeSet` within 1.5x of a packed B-tree); `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-23-decisive-workloads-design.md` has the table.
