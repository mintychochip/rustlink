rustlink benchmark record: node graphs and B-trees
==================================================

The six workloads of `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-23-decisive-workloads-design.md` (ngraph-mesh,
ngraph-banded, ngraph-random, ngraph-chase, btree-64, btree-256), measured on 2026-10-23 after the
first record (`bench/`). Predictions P1-P8 were written before the first measurement; their outcome
is the last section of that note.

Files
  results.jsonl    One row per cell (364): 303 done, 61 unreachable. Every row is a verbatim line written
                   by `sweep`, from the sweep named in replicates.tsv (column chosen_replicate). Registry
                   order.
  results.md       The report, rendered from results.jsonl with `sweep --report-only`. Each cell shows the
                   value of one sweep (the median among the calm sweeps), a different sweep for every cell,
                   so compare variants with ratios.tsv and do not divide two cells of results.md. In its
                   header the git revision is the checkout the binaries were built from (5649039) and
                   MemAvailable is the value when the report was rendered.
  replicates.tsv   Per done cell (303): traversal-time statistics over the calm sweeps and over all 37
                   sweeps, the sweep results.jsonl uses, and every sweep's value (replicate:ns). Together
                   with sweeps.tsv it is enough to re-derive ratios.tsv and results.jsonl's selection.
  sweeps.tsv       Per sweep (222 = 37 replicates x 6 workloads): the canary inflation, whether the sweep
                   counts as calm, and the load while it ran (busy cores on the other CPUs, seconds lost
                   on cpu2, cpu2's mean frequency).
  ratios.tsv       Paired ratios, variant time / base time within one sweep: median, interquartile range
                   and extremes over the calm sweeps, median and interquartile range over all sweeps. Bases:
                   u32, usize, and rc (node graphs) or std (B-trees).

Machine and settings
  CPU          Intel Core Ultra 7 270K Plus, 24 CPUs, no SMT; every cell pinned to cpu2 (the CPU with the
               highest cpuinfo_max_freq). Caches of cpu2: L1d 48K, L2 3 MiB, L3 36 MiB shared. Kernel
               7.1.10-200.fc44.x86_64. Compiler rustc 1.99.0-nightly (6f72b5dd5 2026-07-22), release
               profile (opt-level 3, fat LTO). Power as in bench/environment.txt (powersave governor,
               balance_performance, turbo on); cpu2 averaged 5.38 to 5.40 GHz over the sweeps.
  Binaries     `sweep` and `rlb` built from 5649039 and not rebuilt during the measurements.
  Matrix       364 cells: the four node-graph workloads have 10 variants (rc, usize, u32, abs32, abs32-idx,
               abs16, rel32, rel16, rel16-idx, rel8), the two B-tree workloads 6 (std, box, usize, u32,
               abs32, abs16), each at 2^10 to 2^22 elements in steps of 2^2 (7 sizes). Seed 1, 6
               repetitions per cell (1 discarded warm-up and 5 measured, inside one fresh process).
               `abs32-idx` and `rel16-idx` are probes that exist only in the benchmark crate: they wrap
               the same arenas as `abs32` and `rel16` but decode with the public `Link::target` and index
               the slice themselves, without `Cursor`, to tell the cost of `Cursor` from the cost of the
               decode.
  Units        edge (ngraph-mesh, -banded, -random sweeps), step (ngraph-chase), lookup (B-trees);
               heap bytes per node or, for B-trees, per key.

Why 37 replicates, and how they are judged
  The machine is a desktop shared with other sessions that build, test and run servers (see "Load"). The
  first record's rule (the fastest measurement taken while few other CPUs were busy) cannot be met: the
  busy-core count stopped predicting the slowdown. So every workload was measured in 37 complete sweeps
  of its matrix, `sweep --workloads W` once per workload per replicate, the workload order rotated per
  replicate, and the ratio between two variants is taken inside one sweep, where both ran within seconds
  of each other under the same load. A cell's time is the median over sweeps, a ratio the median of the
  per-sweep ratios.

  Canaries: before and after every workload's sweep, three cells of the first record whose quiet
  values are known were measured on cpu2 (graph-linked-seq u32 at 2^20, quiet 13.0 ns; graph-linked-shuf
  u32 at 2^20, 25.8 ns; graph-linked-seq usize at 2^20, 19.37 ns). A canary reading is the mean of
  (time now / quiet time) over the three cells; a sweep's inflation is the mean of its two bracketing
  readings. A sweep is "calm" when its inflation is below 1.5. The tables in the README and the
  statistics columns headed calm use the calm sweeps: 91 of 222 (ngraph-mesh 16, -banded 15, -random 16,
  -chase 14, btree-64 15, btree-256 15). Calm sweeps still read 1.17 to 1.49 times the quiet values
  (median 1.37): the machine was never quiet. The limit of 1.5 was fixed after the per-sweep inflations
  and a first table of ratios by inflation band (24 replicates) had been seen, so that every workload
  keeps at least 14 calm sweeps; it is the same for every cell and variant, and the next-but-one section
  shows the headline ratios under other limits.

  replicates  window (2026-10-23)  calm sweeps  how they ran
  1-11        14:11-14:38          33 of 66      one replicate after another

  Replicate 12 was first interrupted by a full disk; its partial files were deleted and it ran again from
  the start. An earlier partial set without the two probe variants is not part of the record.

  results.jsonl keeps, for every done cell, the whole row of the calm sweep with the median traversal
  time (the lower median for an even count, ties to the lower replicate). Unreachable cells only report
  link distances and are deterministic; their rows come from replicate 1. Absolute times are therefore
  the median of 14 to 16 calm sweeps, about 1.4 times slower than the quiet machine of the first record,
  and cannot be compared with bench/results.md; ratios can.

  What holds still and what moves (max / min of a ratio over the four columns, from the table above and
  from the same computation for other cells). Ratios between variants of similar node size away from a
  cache boundary hold still: banded `rel16` / `u32` is 0.78 to 0.85 in every band, mesh `rel16` / `u32`
  at 2^16 is 2.16 to 2.17, and the B-tree ratios at 2^16 and 2^18 agree within 2% in the bands <1.3,
  <1.5 and all 37 (the heaviest band reads 6% to 10% lower for `usize`, `abs32` and `BTreeSet`). Ratios
  against a larger footprint, and cells near a cache boundary, move with the load: mesh `rc` / `u32` at
  2^16 is 1.91 to 1.95 until the heaviest band (3.04); mesh `abs32` / `u32` at 2^22 falls from 2.16 to
  1.49; the chases at 2^20 fall toward 1 (`rc` 1.51 to 1.09, `usize` 1.45 to 1.07) and at 2^18 rise
  (`usize` 1.98 to 3.60); btree-256 `u32` / `usize` at 2^22 goes from 0.79 to 0.98. The likely reason
  (not measured) is the shared L3: under load a footprint that fitted it spills to DRAM, which pulls
  the 2^20 and 2^22 ratios toward 1, and the larger footprint at 2^18 suffers more. Verdicts by band:
  the falsifications of P3 and P8 hold in every band shown, except btree-64 `std` at 2^20 in the
  heaviest band (1.43, inside P8's 1.5); P2 on the random graph holds in every band; P2 on the chase
  (1.09 to 1.51), P4 (1.07 to 1.45) and P6's 256-byte bound at 2^22 (0.79 to 0.98) cross their
  thresholds depending on the band. The chase ratios at 2^20 against inflation (all 37 sweeps,
  Spearman -0.46 for usize/u32, -0.69 for rc/u32) extrapolate linearly to 1.31 and 1.41 at
  inflation 1.0. That is an extrapolation and was not measured: the record's 1.22 and 1.29 are
  probably conservative, and neither reaches the 2.0 that P2 predicted for rc.

Unreachable cells (61, deterministic)
  abs16 19, rel8 26, rel16 8 and rel16-idx 8 cells do not fit their graph. abs16 stops at 2^14 in the four
  graph workloads: an absolute 16-bit link addresses at most 65,535 elements and the next size is 2^16.
  rel8 fits the mesh only up to 2^12 and never the banded or the random graph (the chase walks the random
  graph); rel16 fits the mesh and the banded graph at every size and the random graph up to 2^14 (a
  direct run of `rlb run --workload ngraph-random --variant rel16` fits at n = 2^15 and not at
  2^15 + 1024). In the B-trees abs16 stops when a level has more than 65,535 nodes: btree-64 at 2^20 keys
  (about 69,900 leaves of 15 keys) and btree-256 at 2^22 keys (about 66,600 leaves of 63).

Limits of this record
  * One machine, one pinned core, one seed per cell, and a shared machine that was never quiet. The
    ratios are medians of paired measurements; absolute times are not comparable with bench/results.md.
  * No hardware counters were available (`perf` is not installed), so statements about caches (L2, L3,
    DRAM) are inferences from working-set sizes and from how ratios move with load, not measurements.
  * The B-trees are packed, bulk-loaded and static; std::collections::BTreeSet is a dynamic structure
    with room for insertion and removal, so its time and memory are not those of an equal. No variant
    mutates.
  * The node graphs have exactly four links per node and sweeps read every neighbour's payload; the
    chase reads one link per step chosen from the payload. Other link densities were not measured.

Reproduce
  cargo build --release --bins
  target/release/sweep --workloads ngraph-mesh,ngraph-banded,ngraph-random,ngraph-chase,btree-64,btree-256 \
      --out my.jsonl --md my.md
  Repeat the command (the sweeps take 2 to 4 minutes together), keep the sweeps' files apart, and take
  ratios between variants inside each sweep. Read a reference cell next to every sweep (for example
  `target/release/rlb run --workload graph-linked-seq --variant u32 --n 1048576 --reps 6 --seed 1`
  pinned to the same CPU with taskset, which read 13.0 ns on the quiet machine of the first record) and
  distrust sweeps in which it reads more than 1.5 times that.
