rustlink benchmark record
=========================

Files
  results.jsonl    One row per cell (405). 391 rows are verbatim lines written by `sweep`, taken from the
                   sweep named in replicates.tsv (column chosen_run). The other 14 (`box` in
                   graph-linked-seq and graph-linked-shuf) are `skipped`: see "Skipped cells".
  results.md       The report, rendered from results.jsonl with `sweep --report-only`.
  replicates.tsv   Per measured cell (391 rows): the median traversal time, the background load and the
                   time lost on the pinned CPU for each of the six sweeps, and which sweep results.jsonl
                   uses. The selection below can be re-derived from this file alone.
  box-paired.tsv   Every measurement of the 14 skipped `box` cells, and of the variants they were
                   paired with, taken after the layout fix ("Skipped cells").
  box-refgated.tsv Every attempt of the reference-gated retry for the same cells ("Skipped cells"):
                   the two `usize` readings around each `box` reading, their bound and the load.

Machine and settings
  CPU          Intel Core Ultra 7 270K Plus, 24 CPUs, no SMT; sweeps pinned to cpu2 (the CPU with the
               highest cpuinfo_max_freq, 5.5 GHz). Caches of cpu2: L1d 48K, L2 3 MiB, L3 36 MiB shared.
  Kernel       7.1.10-200.fc44.x86_64
  Compiler     rustc 1.99.0-nightly (6f72b5dd5 2026-07-22), release profile (opt-level 3, fat LTO)
  Power        cpu2 scaling_governor powersave, energy_performance_preference balance_performance,
               intel_pstate no_turbo 0 (turbo on). Not changed.
  Binaries     The six sweeps ran binaries built from 327e31f, which has the old `box` layout in the
               two linked-list graph workloads (see "Skipped cells"). The later runs of the `box`
               cells ran binaries built from 188c47a or later. Those differ from 327e31f only in the
               report renderer (17589d9) and in that layout (188c47a). The git revision in the
               report header is the checkout the report was last rendered from, not the revision of
               the binaries that measured.
  Matrix       The nine workloads that made up the whole matrix then (bst-random, bst-bulk, ast, ast-bytes,
               graph-csr, graph-linked-seq, graph-linked-shuf, blob-load, blob-lookup): 405 cells, seed 1,
               6 repetitions per cell (1 discarded warm-up and 5 measured, inside one fresh process). No
               cell was skipped for memory; 14 were skipped for another reason ("Skipped cells"). The node
               graphs and B-trees added later are registered too, so a plain `sweep` now runs 769 cells;
               their record is bench/decisive/.

Why six sweeps
  The machine is a desktop shared with other sessions that build and test in parallel (load average
  above 30 at times). Background load slows large cells measurably, so one sweep is not a trustworthy
  record. Six complete sweeps of the same matrix and seed were taken, and for each cell the sweep
  taken under the least interference is used.

  cpu2 while the benchmark ran (both sampled from /proc/stat and /proc/schedstat at 10 Hz by a monitor
  pinned to another CPU). The sweep drivers and the monitor were kept off cpu2 with taskset.

Selection rule for a done cell
  A sweep's measurement of a cell is eligible if
    * the other CPUs averaged fewer than 2.0 busy cores over the cell's time window padded by 0.5 s on
      each side, and
    * cpu2 lost at most max(20 ms, 2% of the cell's time) to other tasks.
  results.jsonl holds the eligible measurement with the lowest median traversal time (ties: the
  lower run number). 279 of the 282 done cells have at least one eligible measurement; the other 3
  (bst-random/usize at 2^22, blob-lookup/abs16 at 2^14 and blob-lookup/rel16 at 2^12) use the same
  rule with fewer than 3.0 busy cores. The load of the chosen measurements has a median of 1.43 busy
  cores (90th percentile 1.86) and a maximum of 2.95. Unreachable cells are deterministic (they only
  report link distances); their rows come from the least loaded sweep.

How close the chosen value is to the fastest of the six sweeps
  For the 114 measured cells of 2^18 nodes and more, the chosen value is the fastest of all six sweeps
  in 94 cases, within 3% in 104 and within 5% in 108. Four are more than 10% above it (blob-load/rel32
  at 2^24: 1.44x, bst-random/usize at 2^20: 1.20x, blob-lookup/rel32 at 2^24: 1.17x, blob-load/abs32
  at 2^24: 1.12x); their faster measurements came from sweeps whose load was just over the 2.0 line
  (2.07 to 2.65), so they show the per-process variation, not a failed rule.

Skipped cells: `box` in graph-linked-seq and graph-linked-shuf (14 cells)
  In the six sweeps the pointer variant of these two workloads allocated each vertex's edges one
  vertex at a time, tail first, whatever the plan's slot order. With a shuffled plan 99.9% of the
  chain steps were still adjacent in memory, so `box` measured best-case locality in
  graph-linked-shuf, and in graph-linked-seq it laid each chain out backwards. Commit 188c47a
  allocates every edge in slot order, as the index variants lay out their arrays. The sweeps' rows
  for these cells describe a layout the benchmark no longer has, so they are not used.

  The fixed variant could not be measured under the rule. A collector that started a cell only after
  the other CPUs had been quiet for three seconds made no attempt in 28 minutes. Between 08:28 and
  09:54 each cell was measured 34 times (10 rounds of the 14 cells, then 24 rounds that also measured
  u32, abs32 and, in graph-linked-seq, rel8 at 2^20 and 2^22). In the 24 paired rounds the other CPUs
  averaged a median of 4.2 busy cores (10th to 90th percentile 2.4 to 9.2, lowest 1.8), and the load no
  longer behaved as in the sweeps. The u32 reference measured in the same rounds ran 1.2x to 1.45x
  slower than its quiet value even below 3 busy cores (about 1.06x at 2 to 3 in the sweeps), and its
  fastest measurement was never closer than 1.17x. The other sessions were putting a memory-system
  load on the machine that the count of busy cores does not capture. No absolute `box` time taken
  then is usable, so the cells are skipped instead of holding inflated numbers. To fill them on an
  idle machine:
      target/release/sweep --workloads graph-linked-seq,graph-linked-shuf --variants box
      target/release/sweep --report-only
  (the report keeps the last measurement of a cell).

  A second try replaced the busy-core count by a direct check. Each attempt measured the `usize`
  variant of the same workload and size (it has the same 72 B per vertex as `box`), then `box`, then
  `usize` again, and kept the `box` reading only if both `usize` readings were at or below the larger
  of its slowest eligible reading in the six sweeps and 1.05x its quiet value, and the pinned CPU
  lost at most max(20 ms, 2%) of each measurement to other tasks. The first 60 minutes (10:11 to
  11:11) also waited until the other CPUs had been at most 3 busy for three seconds, and made 2
  attempts. The next 45 minutes did not wait and made 217. Six attempts passed, for cells of 2^16
  nodes or fewer (graph-linked-seq at 2^10, 2^12, 2^14 and 2^16; graph-linked-shuf at 2^14 and
  2^16), and none of the 150 attempts for 2^18, 2^20 and 2^22: there the lowest `usize` readings
  were 1.26x, 1.12x and 1.04x their quiet values (medians 1.92x, 1.28x and 1.13x), with the other
  CPUs at a median of 10.7 busy cores. Every attempt is in box-refgated.tsv. The six passes are not
  used: they cannot complete the cells that carry the README's claims, a row with some cells
  measured under another rule would mislead, and the gate is weak at 2^10, where its bound is 1.5x
  the quiet value.

  Ratios of paired measurements are more robust: `box` and its reference ran within a minute of each
  other under the same load. Median over the 24 rounds of the per-round ratio box time / reference
  time, with the interquartile range (linear interpolation; recompute it from box-paired.tsv):
                graph-linked-seq 2^20   graph-linked-seq 2^22   graph-linked-shuf 2^20   graph-linked-shuf 2^22
    u32         1.42 (1.28-1.63)        0.91 (0.88-0.92)        1.34 (1.21-1.43)         0.81 (0.80-0.83)
    abs32       1.25 (1.12-1.60)        0.89 (0.84-0.92)        1.58 (1.40-1.75)         0.88 (0.87-0.90)
    rel8        1.19 (1.06-1.39)        0.68 (0.66-0.71)        -                        -
  At 2^22 the pointer variant is as fast as, or faster than, the index variants in both layouts; at
  2^20 it is 1.2x to 1.6x slower. Whether contention hurts the larger pointer heap (72 B per vertex
  against 36) more than the index variants is not known, so the quiet ratios may differ.

Reproduce
  cargo build --release --bins && target/release/sweep --out my.jsonl --md my.md   (keep the machine idle)
  That runs all 769 cells of today's registry. For this record's nine workloads alone add
  --workloads bst-random,bst-bulk,ast,ast-bytes,graph-csr,graph-linked-seq,graph-linked-shuf,blob-load,blob-lookup
  Check the load the way this record did: sample /proc/stat (busy jiffies of every CPU except the
  pinned one) and /proc/schedstat (the pinned CPU's rq_run_delay) while it runs. If the machine cannot
  be idle, repeat sweeps and keep the least disturbed measurement per cell, and measure a reference
  cell (for example u32 of the same workload and size) next to anything you want to trust: the count
  of busy cores alone stopped predicting the slowdown from about 09:00 on 2026-10-23.
